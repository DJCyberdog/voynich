# A-03 — Natural-language hypothesis: source-level falsifiability and unit-identification audit

**Date:** 2026-11-09. **Cycle:** one bounded original-paper review, no manuscript reanalysis. **Main verified:** `eee6721615098918cce02bf76e4fdda26db60176`. **Branch:** existing Draft PR #35 only. **Source:** Reddy & Knight, *What We Know About The Voynich Manuscript*, ACL LaTeCH 2011, pp. 78–86, https://aclanthology.org/W11-1511.pdf (directly read 2026-11-09; original peer-reviewed method paper, existing SRC-0024). Companion Bowern & Lindemann 2021 is already registered as SRC-0023 but **not revalidated** in this cycle. No new SRC/HYP/EXP IDs.

## Question and layer distinction
Does the 2011 paper establish a *natural language with unknown orthography* (strong A-03), or does it only document several natural-language-like **transcription statistics** that a structured cipher or pseudotext could also produce? Source is an original scientific paper **about** manuscript transcriptions, not direct Yale manuscript photography. Do not equate Currier transliteration characters with visible ink glyphs, token blanks with linguistic words, statistical model with decoded morphology, or image associations with semantics.

## Independently read evidence and provenance
- The authors explicitly restrict experimental analysis largely to **Currier B Biological and Stars sections**, rather than claiming manuscript-wide unbiased sampling (p. 79–80; PDF lines 64–66). They state 43 pages, 3920 word types, 17597 tokens, 35 transcription characters for their chosen sample. **DOCUMENTED as reported original-paper methods/counts; not VERIFIED by re-running their corpus.**
- Their **2-state character HMM** separated last-character behavior from preceding characters, yielding an `a*b`-type model (p. 80, lines 67–73). The authors propose abjad as *one possible* explanation, comparing vowel-stripped English and Arabic, not as a decipherment. **DOCUMENTED model description; CLAIMED empirical result; language identity UNKNOWN.**
- Their word entropy table gives VMS B **9.666 bits**, English **10.07**, Arabic **9.645**, Chinese **10.31** (p. 80, table 1). Their reported within-word held-out letter-predictability (p. 81, table 2) is **40.02%** for Voynich B bigram and **14.65%** unigram, compared with English 22.62%/11.09%, Arabic 24.78%/13.29%, Pinyin 38.92%/11.20%. These are **author-reported metrics**, not newly measured values. Baselines use different corpora and scripts, limiting linguistic attribution.
- The authors' own conclusion (p. 85, lines 264–267) describes both *linguistically aberrant* features (e.g. letter bigrams/line edges) and *language-like* features (length/frequency, apparent morphology, page topics), explicitly calls for further cross-script comparisons before definite conclusions. Thus the strong reading “2011 establishes natural language” is **CONTRADICTED at source-interpretation level**, **NOT** a refutation of the natural-language possibility.
- Reddy & Knight discuss Cardan-grid-like finite-state word generation and hoax alternatives (p. 85, lines 259–262); such alternatives are compatible with parts of the observed distribution. Source is original author work; no independently verified manuscript text was used in this cycle.

## Strongest alternative and planned discrimination (NOT an executed experiment)
**H1:** A fixed, productive linguistic morphological/context model provides reproducible **additional** heldout predictive information about word relationships that cannot be explained by purely position/section/hand-conditioned form generation.
**H0/strong counter:** A nonsemantic positional finite-state / self-copying model, or a cipher/hybrid using a comparable complexity budget, reproduces the same word-length, word-type and local glyph-context patterns; hence those patterns are insufficient to identify natural language.

**Preregistration required before any test:** independently version-pin and rights-audit a named ZL3b-n IVTFF witness and its comma/dot policy, plus a second transcription witness; define folio/quire-heldout splits, section/Currier A/B and scribal covariates; freeze word-unit definition, feature sets, smoothing, capacity budget, metric (heldout predictive log-loss), a practical minimum improvement and uncertainty method before looking at outcomes. Compare three model families: (i) linguistically motivated morpheme+context, (ii) positional template / copying without meaning, (iii) cipher/hybrid preserving boundary and positional constraints. Each receives equal training information and parameter tuning budget; do not tune on heldout folios.

**Positive controls:** apply identical pipeline to version-pinned *known* natural-language prose and a fixed reversible encoding, to verify that known linguistic structure can be recovered under this protocol; these are sanity checks, not a proof for MS408.
**Negative controls:** position-, length-, hand-, section- and folio-preserving surrogate sequences; randomised local context **without destroying the confounding structure**, plus a synthetic grammar with known nonsemantic generation. Report multiple comparisons and corpus-coverage uncertainty.
**Heldout:** unseen entire folios or quires, not intermingled lines; separate manuscripts/sections only when genuine independent samples exist. If data or rights insufficient, status BLOCKED for the actual test, not a fabricated null result.
**Falsification:** If pre-registered linguistic features show no material additional heldout performance beyond the strongest nonlinguistic/cipher controls, the *strong discriminating A-03 prediction* fails in the tested scope; it does **not** disprove every conceivable natural-language encoding. If they do outperform, only additional structured prediction is established, not natural-language identity or semantic decipherment.
