## Question and discriminatory finding

**Question:** Does the Naibbe cipher's published fit to Voynich B independently validate a historical homophonic cipher explanation for MS408?

**Original publication:** Michael A. Greshko, *The Naibbe cipher: a substitution cipher that encrypts Latin and Italian as Voynich Manuscript-like ciphertext*, *Cryptologia*, published online **2025-11-26**, DOI https://doi.org/10.1080/01611194.2025.2566408 (publisher full text directly reviewed 2026-11-09). Data/code links in original: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.16415087 and https://github.com/greshko/naibbe-cipher . **Not fetched or executed**; licenses of individual data/code/transcriptions remain to be checked before reuse.

The author explicitly describes (1) a reversible verbose homophonic cipher for Latin/Italian, hand-executable with period-plausible materials; (2) unigram plaintext letters encoded as entire Voynich-like words and bigram positions as prefixes/suffixes; (3) six substitution tables; (4) **affix strings and their frequency-rank assignments derived from Voynich A/B VT transliteration**, with the 138 most common first and second affixes of Voynich B used as assignments; (5) tuning of table assignments and the approximately 50/50 unigram-bigram balance plus 5:2:2:2:1:1 table ratios to match **Voynich B's observed frequency-rank distribution**. The paper explicitly says its goal is compatibility of a *class of possible ciphers*, not deciphering MS408.

**Material distinction:** The reported similarity of generated word frequencies and affix frequencies to Voynich B is not an *independent held-out validation* of Naibbe as the manuscript's historical mechanism, because the published cipher's tables and several structural parameters were selected using Voynich B. This is a transparent, legitimate *constructive existence demonstration*; it is not an accusation of leakage misconduct. The paper does include multiple Latin/Italian plaintext encryptions, which test stability across plaintexts **conditional on the Voynich-fitted cipher**. Do not conflate this with holding out manuscript folios for validation.

## Source-specific falsification and alternatives

**Narrow falsifiable attribution:** “The Naibbe word/affix-frequency fit to Voynich B was measured without any parameter choice informed by Voynich B.” **CONTRADICTED** by the author's explicit table-building and tuning description. This refutes *only that independence claim*, not the feasibility or the original author claim.

**Strongest counterhypothesis:** A generator trained or hand-tuned on Voynich B (whether a homophonic cipher, self-copy process, grille or hybrid) can reproduce selected B distributions without having generated the historical manuscript. A natural language with unknown orthography also remains open. The publication does not establish a manuscript plaintext or uniquely favor one mechanism.

**Preregistered follow-up (NOT RUN):** Freeze the exact published six tables, weighting, plaintext preprocessing and code commit; document VT witness/version, rights, and any tuning set. Compare predictions on **untouched manuscript folios/section A**, with explicit stratification by section and scribal hand, against (i) original Naibbe fitted cipher, (ii) same-class untuned randomized affix tables and alternative historical plaintexts, (iii) matched self-copy and grille controls, (iv) natural-language/orthographic baseline. Positive control: encrypt/decrypt new known Latin/Italian text with exact frozen tables and verify round-trip. Negative control: frequency-matched shuffled manuscript tokens and/or randomized cipher assignments preserving marginal counts. Null: performance no better than equally parameterized, Voynich-fitted alternative generators on held-out structural diagnostics (position, cross-boundary, higher-order dependencies). Choose effect sizes, sample sizes, metrics and correction for multiple tests **before** access to holdout; prohibit using test folios to re-fit any parameter. A Voynich-B-specific cipher might legitimately fail on Currier A, so scope any negative result accordingly. Preserve images → glyph reading → VT/EVA witness → tokenization → statistics → semantic inference boundaries.
