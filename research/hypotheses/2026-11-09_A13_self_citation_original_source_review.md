## Strongest counterhypothesis and model comparison
A language, cipher or hybrid with repeated morphemes, formulaic phrases or scribal copying could generate locally similar tokens and Zipf-like frequency patterns **without** the text being meaningless. Conversely a nonsemantic self-citation generator can generate some such patterns without being the historical process. Model fit is not provenance proof.

## Falsification design (NOT RUN)
- Freeze author algorithm, exact source commit, seed, settings and rights-reviewed manuscript transcription(s); never use evaluation folios to tune.
- **Positive control:** independently reproduce author-reported output diagnostics on author-provided generated output and its stated analysis corpus, without treating it as an independent manuscript success.
- **Negative controls:** word-frequency-preserving token shuffles, folio/line-local order shuffles, and an explicit matched language/cipher or hybrid baseline; ensure identical segmentation and line-position treatment.
- **Discriminating outcomes:** preselect joint held-out statistics (local edit-distance/recurrence conditioned on position, prefix/suffix directionality, line boundaries, section differences, novel-token distribution), not just two Zipf-law curves; report confidence intervals and multiple comparisons. If a frozen self-citation model fails predeclared tolerances against the held-out manuscript while competing controls pass, the **specified model** is contradicted within that scope; variants not tested remain open.
- **Heldout:** folios and sections selected *before* model tuning, with independent transcript witness if permitted; no retroactive threshold selection.
- **Null:** multiple unrelated generation mechanisms produce superficially similar Zipf/token-network signatures. Such signatures alone do not identify historical authorship or semantics.
