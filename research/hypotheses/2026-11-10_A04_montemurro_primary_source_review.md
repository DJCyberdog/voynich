## Primary publication and precise claim
Marcelo A. Montemurro and Damián H. Zanette, *Keywords and Co-Occurrence Patterns in the Voynich Manuscript: An Information-Theoretic Analysis*, PLOS ONE 8(6):e66344 (published 2013-06-21), https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0066344 (consulted 2026-11-10). The authors compute word-position/section information measures and co-occurrence networks, compare with natural-language texts and globally shuffled word positions, and interpret the observed nonuniformity as compatible with an informative message. The published article is CC BY (attribution required). Its text and methods are accessible; **the underlying manuscript transcription's exact bytes, license, version and folio alignment are not thereby authenticated or cleared for redistribution**. No manuscript images or corpus bytes copied.

**Critical distinction:** the reported statistical nonuniformity is not equivalent to demonstrated semantics. A global shuffle that destroys section and folio composition is a weak null for a manuscript already organized into illustrated sections, different hands and layouts. The paper's semantic-network labels are an interpretive inference from distributional association, not independently read word meanings. Its own discussion acknowledges that the origins and meaning remain unresolved. This is a methodological limitation, **not** an empirical refutation of the original statistic.

## Falsifiable next comparison (design only; not preregistered as an executed experiment)
**Question:** Does long-range word concentration survive controls that retain section/folio/scribe/layout structure and transcription uncertainty, beyond a globally shuffled null?

**Competing explanations:** (i) language or meaningful coded/hybrid text with topic-conditioned lexical choices; (ii) nonsemantic copying or constrained generator with section-conditioned token inventories; (iii) different scribes, folio sections, layouts and editorial segmentation. No origin mechanism is selected a priori.

**Prediction:** A genuine cross-folio topical effect should yield incremental held-out predictive value for section labels after conditioning on scribe, folio, token length, local token inventory and transcription convention, and outperform matched nonsemantic section-conditioned generators; merely beating global shuffling is insufficient. **Falsifier:** effect disappears or matches section-conditioned controls on held-out folios and independent transcription witnesses. The reverse is evidence for organization only, not meaning.

**Controls:** positive: known section-structured natural-language text, processed identically; negative: section-conditioned copy generator and matched permutations within predeclared folio/scribe/length strata; baseline: original global shuffle for reproduction only. Hold out whole folios before feature selection, prevent cross-fold reuse of overlapping lines; prespecify stratification and avoid impossible within-stratum contrasts.
