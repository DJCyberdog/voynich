## Primary author document and claim
Stephen Bax, *A proposed partial decoding of the Voynich script*, **Version 1, January 2014**, https://stephenbax.net/wp-content/uploads/2014/01/Voynich-a-provisional-partial-decoding-BAX.pdf (original author-hosted PDF, inspected 2026-11-10). Abstract pp. 1 and introduction p. 5: proposes **ten** provisional readings based on **one constellation and seven plants**, involving **fourteen signs and clusters**; Appendix 1 pp. 56–57 gives proposed sound correspondences. The document itself labels the work *proposed* and *partial*. Its f68r Pleiades/Taurus and f15v–f16r juniper readings are explicit case studies. Bax explicitly calls the initial /arar/ and /taərn/ identifications individually insubstantial and speculative (p. 21); this caveat must travel with all reported cribs. The paper also asserts non-hoax/natural-language implications, but these are **author interpretations**, not verified entailments.

## Discriminating question
Can a **frozen** mapping derived exclusively from Bax's published training examples predict phonetic/lexical readings for previously unseen image-identified objects or labels at a rate exceeding a matched flexible-crib null, without adjusting plant IDs, sound values or spellings after inspection?

**Prediction / falsification (prospective, NOT RUN):** Freeze exact glyph-to-sound table, allowed variants, original image loci and transcription convention; blind annotators classify object identities independently of label strings. On folio-disjoint heldout illustrations, score predeclared exact/phonological matches against a fixed historical name list. If the fixed mapping fails to outperform predeclared shuffled-label/alternative-object nulls under multiplicity correction, reject its *out-of-sample predictive advantage* (not all possible phonetic interpretations).

**Strongest alternative:** post-hoc matching between ambiguous botanical depictions, flexible medieval name variants and adjustable phonetic assignments. Also compare an encrypted-language hypothesis and nonlinguistic/hybrid label generator under identical heldout criteria. Positive control: known medieval labeled herbal with a known script and object taxonomy; negative controls: permuted labels and visually mismatched plants; null: conditional label permutations preserving section, length and glyph uncertainty. Holdout: folios not used in Bax's ten proposed readings, fixed before opening their transcriptions. Include uncertainty from image strokes -> graphemes -> EVA/IVTFF -> segmentation -> statistics; no semantic equivalence inferred from spelling alone.
