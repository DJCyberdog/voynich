## New original source (published, directly inspected)

Colin Layfield and Lisa Fagin Davis, **“Singulion Structure and the Voynich Manuscript,”** *Digital Medievalist* 19 (2026), DOI **https://doi.org/10.4000/16k0a** (publisher full text). Companion source: Layfield and Davis, “The Application of Latent Semantic Analysis to the Voynich Manuscript,” *Digital Humanities Quarterly* 20(1), DOI https://doi.org/10.63744/2ezxpskcezq4 (only referenced here; no new independent replication). The new publisher paper uses LSA similarities among **confoliate**, **conjoint** and **facing** manuscript pages and argues for originally loose single-bifolium **singulions** rather than nested quires. It analyzes physical page adjacency, not semantic translation. Its proposed reorderings particularly concern balneological **ff.75–84** and marginal-star **ff.103–116**. The authors explicitly acknowledge limited-vocabulary/repetitive-text and chance alternatives, and say the intended order cannot be known for sure without reading the text.

**Specific new confound for A-01:** A/B text regimes, scribal hands, physical bifolia, quire position and modern page order must be treated as *separate, potentially correlated variables*. If page adjacency or physical bifolium organization influences lexical similarity, A/B differences alone cannot identify two languages or two scribes. This is a **design constraint inferred from the primary paper**, NOT an empirical rejection of Currier A/B, nor a verification of singulions. A-02 shares the confound; it does not gain a second independent finding.
