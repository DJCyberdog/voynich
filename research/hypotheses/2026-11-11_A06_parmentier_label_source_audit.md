## Original source and exact claim

François Parmentier, *Deciphering the Voynich manuscript: propositions to unlock research*, published online July 2021, *Cryptologia* 46(5):461–484, DOI https://doi.org/10.1080/01611194.2021.1919944. Author-uploaded full-text https://www.researchgate.net/publication/353715072_Deciphering_the_Voynich_manuscript_propositions_to_unlock_research, section II.2 pp. 16–19 (accessed 2026-11-11). Source is copyrighted author-uploaded text; **no copy of PDF, images, glyphs or manuscript transcript committed**. The text of the source was inspected, not its supplementary label dataset or R code; author's measurements are **DOCUMENTED author claims, not VERIFIED independently**.

The author defined a label as an inscription of fewer than three words beside an illustration and outside a paragraph. **48 pages, 729 manually transcribed labels**; **569 distinct words submitted to the matching algorithm** (because of redundancy), **249/569 = 43.8%** reported hapax. IMPORTANT: 43.8% denominator is **569**, not 729; labels and distinct tested word types are different units. Author reports Wilcoxon W=51962 p<.001 and after outlier removal W=27454 p<.001; **neither independently recomputed**. Author reports EVA-like `otaiin` beside f71v and about 175 occurrences manuscript-wide; **not independently counted**. Figure 6 names alleged cross-object label repetitions including f70v2.S2.14 / f72v1.S1.4 / f99r.P1.4 / f99r.L2.5a; no visual equality or object-identity independently confirmed. The original source explicitly allows alternate explanations: descriptive captions, common adjectives, shared object properties, abjad collisions, many-to-one ciphers, or text/image independence.

**Claim under audit:** the *universal naive model* “every short nearby string is a unique object name, with one stable unambiguous orthographic mapping” is questioned by reported reuse. This **does not** prove that labels have no meaning, that none is a proper name, that all names must be unique, or that the manuscript is nonlinguistic. Label recurrence in running prose is expected for real names and therefore **not a falsification by itself**.

## Prospective discriminating replication contract (not executed)

**Loci/data layers:** source author example f71v and cross-domain loci f70v2.S2.14, f72v1.S1.4, f99r.P1.4, f99r.L2.5a. Owner-canonical IIIF SRC-0007 may index canvas IDs; mapping to physical folios, illustrated object identity and paleographic glyph readings must be independently spot-checked. Keep visible image, independent glyph reading, EVA/IVTFF version, word segmentation, source-author string matching, and semantic object annotation separate.

**Prediction:** after independent image/glyph reading and a frozen deterministic exact-match rule, multiple labels assigned to independently *different object categories* share identical transcription strings more often than a single-unambiguous-name model with category-conditioned label frequencies predicts. Fix which cases count as different objects **before** text reading.

**Strongest counterhypothesis:** shared strings denote legitimate category words, attributes, grammatical forms, repeated proper names, abbreviation/abjad collisions or many-to-one cipher outputs; or transcription errors and glyph segmentation collapse distinct strings.

**Positive control:** visually and palaeographically verified identical glyph strings assigned to two objects in same category, with deliberately planted known duplicate string pairs in a separate synthetic annotation exercise (to validate the matching pipeline, not historical semantics). **Negative control:** random category reassignment among image objects within comparable section/folio and held-out string pairs with deliberately altered one glyph to verify exact matching rejects near-matches. **Null:** section/folio/length-conditioned permutation of string-to-object pairings preserving token multiplicity and label count, compared with a frozen model of one-to-one unique names; do not use naive global shuffle across heterogeneous sections. **Heldout:** entire as-yet-uninspected label-bearing folios selected and frozen *before* annotation; f71v and figure-6 examples are development examples and **must never be called independent heldout**. Pre-register threshold, sample size, exclusion of ambiguous glyphs and statistical test before images/data inspection.

**Falsifier:** under the exact one-to-one model, even one independently established identical inscription mapped to two unquestionably distinct referents violates the *universal uniqueness assumption*; this does not falsify natural language, cipher, mixed or nonlanguage families. Under a probabilistic weaker naming model, compare held-out conditional rates and uncertainty intervals against alternative models; cannot reject without measured data.
