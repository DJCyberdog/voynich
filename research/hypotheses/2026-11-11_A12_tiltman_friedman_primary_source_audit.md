## Narrow finding
Tiltman explicitly attributes to Friedman the belief that Voynich script could be a primitive synthetic universal language. Tiltman himself found Wilkins/Dalgarno too systematic and described the text instead as an awkward mixture of substitutions. His tentative root/suffix and A/O group observations were based on a small photostat sample and his own arbitrary 17-symbol transcription, with acknowledged uncertainties in glyph boundaries and spaces. Tiltman's comparison to Cave Beck's *Universal Character* (1657) demonstrates a possible **analogy**, not a historically viable source for a fifteenth-century manuscript. The 1657 and later examples cannot establish the origin of MS408. **DOCUMENTED:** what Tiltman wrote, historical comparison, and his qualifications. **CLAIMED:** Friedman's conjecture as reported by Tiltman. **UNKNOWN:** a constructed language in MS408; actual compositional semantics and historical origin. **INFERRED:** slot regularity alone is not discriminative between semantic constructed language, hybrid cipher, and nonsemantic production.

Compare with natural-language and cipher baselines using identical sampling and tokenization. **Falsifier:** semantic predictions do not exceed blinded controls on heldout, while matched generators fit equally well. This would reject only the specified compositional model, not every conceivable constructed language.
