## One sharply testable subclaim and preregistered future test
**Claim selected:** no double consonants in the author's purported phonemic/letter representation. The exact source phrase is an author's assertion; do not equate it to EVA repeated ASCII characters. **Prediction, to freeze before data:** given a complete version-pinned author's original glyph-to-letter table and unambiguous glyph segmentation, the author-defined letter stream has zero adjacent identical consonant letters (after explicitly frozen word boundaries and abbreviation expansions). **Falsifier:** one independently read, uncontested original glyph pair mapped by frozen rules to the same consonant twice in a single word (unless a prior written exception is part of the model). **Strongest alternative:** the author's visual segmentation and proposed transliteration are flexible enough to suppress consonant doubling; a different historical language, cipher, constructed/nonlanguage or hybrid process can also produce few/no double consonants. This is a necessary-feature test, **not** a unique Romance-language identifier.

**Controls:** positive = an independently segmented known text from an orthographic system with no double consonants under the same algorithm; negative = a documented doubled-consonant corpus, including hand-inserted within-word doubled consonant exemplars. **Null:** randomize or independently model consonant adjacency conditional on word length and consonant inventory, with matched script/genre baselines; no arbitrary uniform-random-text null. **Heldout:** freeze a folio-stratified set of manuscript folios not used in selecting letter values; cannot yet attest author-unseen material. **Unit and provenance:** manuscript image/physical glyph observation -> segmentation -> author-defined grapheme -> proposed consonant classification -> adjacency count.
