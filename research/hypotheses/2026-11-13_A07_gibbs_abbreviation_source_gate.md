## Falsifiable prediction / alternatives (DESIGN ONLY; NOT preregistered or executed)
**Gibbs-specific strong form:** a single frozen mapping from individually audited MS408 glyph/allograph patterns to medieval Latin abbreviation expansions should yield grammatical Latin and coherent, prospective ingredient/procedure sequences on **unseen** manuscript regions, without missing-index rescue or image-guided lexical substitution.

**Strongest counterhypothesis:** arbitrary visual resemblance to common Latin abbreviation signs, plus free expansion and selection of illustrated medicinal context, can produce cherry-picked Latin phrases even when the underlying system is nonlinguistic, ciphered, another language or hybrid. The broader historical existence of Latin abbreviations does NOT uniquely favor Gibbs's particular solution.

**Proposed preregistered test if source and rights gates clear:** select one illustration-independent botanical folio and one bath/recipe folio as *development*, freeze source image region coordinates, independent stroke readings, abbreviation table, allowed expansion variants, morphological grammar, and scoring protocol; blind two disjoint unseen folios (one non-botanical) for two independent Latin specialists. Compare (a) frozen Gibbs expansion, (b) matched-budget alternative abbreviation mappings, (c) simple Latin/other-language cipher candidates, and (d) nonsemantic word-generator controls. Positive control: a documented 15th-century Latin manuscript with verified expansions and equivalent abbreviation density, hidden from raters. Negative control: visually similar non-Latin glyph strings or shuffled mappings with matched token length/position. Null: chance/overfit success of same-parameter expansion families, evaluated by preregistered grammaticality and unambiguous passage-level prediction. Stratify by folio/section and holdout, do not train on heldout. No thresholds or data partitions frozen today, so no claim of a completed preregistration.
