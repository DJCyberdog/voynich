### Claim vs actual technical specification
The author **CLAIMS** a hand-executable verbose homophonic substitution cipher capable of encrypting Latin and Italian into fully decryptable text reproducing several Voynich-like distributions. This is a constructive *compatibility/existence* result, not a decryption of MS 408 and not proof of historical use.

**DOCUMENTED from publisher text:** respacing into plaintext unigrams and bigrams; six substitution tables (one primary, three secondary, two tertiary); each plaintext letter's representation depends on unigram, first-bigram, second-bigram role; unigram letter -> whole ciphertext word, bigram first -> prefix, second -> suffix. Word assignments are *partly calibrated against Voynich A/B word types and their frequencies*; the 138 commonest first and second affixes in Voynich B are used for prefix/suffix assignments. The author explicitly states a **roughly 50/50 unigram/bigram split and 5:2:2:2:1:1 table ratios** were derived from fitting Voynich B's common word frequency ranks. Thus in-sample resemblance is **not independent held-out evidence** of historical mechanism.

**CLAIMED quantitative observations from paper, NOT reproduced here:** model variants with >~3% plaintext trigrams overproduce long word types under the reported settings; commonest model token frequency 2.3 ± 0.1% vs 2.1% cited for Voynich B; model produces a Voynich-B-like Heaps/Zipf pattern. The cited 500+500 synthetic model runs, 5 million model tokens and R²~0.9 are author claims, not new MS408 measurements. No new Voynich frequency values are independently computed.

## Predeclared next falsification (NOT EXECUTED)
**Discriminating question:** Can an author-frozen Naibbe encoder trained/calibrated solely on Voynich B training folios, without test-folio or cross-transcription leakage, predict *joint* word-length, line-position, suffix-class transitions and vocabulary statistics on unseen folios better than (i) a similarly tuned self-copying nonsemantic generator, (ii) a natural-language orthographic baseline, and (iii) a hybrid generator with equal tuning budget?

**H1:** A frozen Naibbe mechanism yields better held-out joint predictive fit than equal-budget alternatives under a predeclared score; its ability to encode/decode arbitrary synthetic Latin/Italian must be checked separately. **H0:** after calibrating on Voynich B, Naibbe's held-out score is no better than competing generators. **Strongest counterhypothesis:** deliberate affix-table tuning to the manuscript reproduces training distributions regardless of whether MS408 is encoded text. Even H1 would not identify Naibbe historically or decipher MS408.

**Positive control:** independently chosen Latin/Italian plaintexts encoded and then decoded by the frozen author's algorithm, with exact letter-order recovery; separately check that a known-generated ciphertext can be recognized. **Negative controls:** prefix/suffix assignments shuffled with preserved length/frequency, plus matched self-copying nonsemantic generator. **Null:** training-only matched-generator distributions; all hyperparameters fixed before held-out evaluation. **Heldout:** complete folios, preferably distinct Currier B sections/scribes, preselected before fitting, with alternative independent transcription witness; no leakage via author's prefit VT tables (must first determine whether truly untrained folios exist). **Failure threshold:** preregister effect size, joint scoring rule, folds and multiplicity only after exact accessible data and fair model implementations are available. **BLOCKER:** author affix assignments already derived from Voynich B; cannot honestly call any unspecified Voynich B folio pristine without a provenance-level table training split. No new statistical test conducted and no invented p-values.
