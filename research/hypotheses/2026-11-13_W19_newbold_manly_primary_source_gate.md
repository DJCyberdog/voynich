## Falsifiable core and strongest counterhypothesis
W-19 historical hypothesis: a stable microscopic second script exists *within* visible ink glyphs and yields a reproducible mapping, independently of a reader's preferred Latin text. **Strongest alternative:** pigment cracking, surface texture, scanning/compression and reader-dependent pareidolia generate apparent small marks; arbitrary micro-mark assignment and anagramming permit post-hoc Latin.

**Pretest specification, not preregistered as a performed experiment:** identify exact Newbold plate/folio/region coordinates and primary mapping; freeze imaging scale, acquisition method, micro-mark segmentation and glyph-to-sign conversion on training regions. Before any heldout reveal, lock a finite alphabet and deterministic decoding order. Have at least two blind annotators independently mark (a) original Yale regions, (b) similar-age ink/parchment controls, (c) synthetic embedded microtext positive controls, and (d) matched random crack/noise negatives. Test agreement, image-resampling stability, out-of-sample symbol assignments and a unique Latin output on untouched regions. Null: local ink-surface texture and crack morphology conditional on stroke width/pigment density. Require a declared threshold and historical controls before any confirmatory inference. Repeated non-identical decoding of the same region or inability to discriminate crack controls would falsify the *specific fixed microshorthand implementation*, not every possible cryptosystem.
